# F-23 — The paper reads its correlation heatmap as physical coupling, but the sizes in it are set by how much scatter each sensor carries

**Established from.** Wen et al. (2026), the copy in `docs/references/`
(`wen_2026_estgnn_dynamic_shap.pdf`, SHA-256 beginning `ef660c23`), page 8
rendered at eight times its size with `pypdfium2` and read on 2026-11-14, and
the 20,631 rows of `train_FD001.txt`, measured the same day. It was found while
drawing the sensors' correlations on the C-MAPSS resource page, which the
author asked for that day; the page's section "What the FD001 training engines
look like" teaches every step below with the real numbers.

**What the paper prints.**

- Section 2.3.1 (p. 5): the entries of its sensor graph "are set to 1 for
  strong physical relationships between sensor pairs and 0 otherwise".
- Section 3.2.2 (p. 8) reads its Fig. 5 as showing such relationships:
  temperature sensors (T24, T30, T50) "demonstrating strong intercorrelations
  due to thermodynamic coupling throughout the engine cycle"; "Pressure
  measurements (P15, P30, Ps30) exhibit similar correlation patterns
  reflecting the progressive compression process"; "Rotational speed
  parameters (Nf, Nc, NRf, NRc) show high correlation coefficients, indicating
  mechanical coupling through shared shaft systems."
- Fig. 5 (p. 8), "Correlation heatmap of FD001 dataset after removing the
  constant features", has 18 rows and columns: engine, cycle, setting_1,
  setting_2 and the 14 sensors of Table 3, each value printed to two decimals.

**What Fig. 5 is.** All 153 values it prints below its diagonal equal the
Pearson correlations of the raw rows of `train_FD001.txt`, the 100 engines
pooled, rounded to two decimals, including the sign of the five printed as
−0.00. On the same rows smoothed with a weight of 0.5 (assumption A-04), none
of its 91 pairs of sensors agrees: T24 with T30, printed 0.60, becomes 0.81. So
the figure was computed from the training rows as recorded, before the
smoothing of Section 3.2.3. NRc is not in it, although it is not constant
(assumption A-10).

**What its numbers measure.** Think of each reading as a slow part, which
FD001's single fault moves over a life, plus a scatter drawn afresh at every
flight. That split is this project's model; the first two measurements below
are its checks.

1. From one flight to the next, the 91 pairs of sensors correlate at 0.011354
   on average, ignoring the sign, and at most 0.047887 (Nf with NRf); over
   whole lives the average is 0.569119. What the sensors share moves slowly;
   from one flight to the next each moves on its own.
2. Each sensor's change from one flight to the next correlates with its
   following change at between −0.509910 and −0.486944. Scatter that is
   unrelated from one flight to the next gives exactly −0.5, and then the
   scatter's variance is half the variance of the change.
3. The share of slow drift, one minus the scatter's variance divided by the
   readings' variance over the file, runs from 0.574262 (T30) to 0.964263
   (Nc), and is 0.077210 for P15.
4. With scatter unrelated to everything else, the correlation of two readings
   is the correlation of their slow parts times the square root of the two
   shares, which this project calls the noise limit. For the 66 pairs that
   involve neither Nc nor P15, the measured size divided by the limit runs from
   0.874844 to 1.012246; the 20 lowest are the pairs that join Nf or NRf to one
   of the ten other sensors, all below 0.97. The slow parts of these twelve
   sensors move almost in step, and the size of a correlation among them is
   set by the scatter each carries.
5. Nc's last 20 readings average higher than its first 20 on 71 engines and
   lower on 29, and its correlation with T50 within each engine is positive on
   exactly those 71. Pooled, its correlations are 0.036129 to 0.434012 of their
   limits. P15 takes two values only, 21.60 and 21.61 (finding F-01).

**What follows for the paper's three readings.**

- Temperatures: the three pairs sit at their limits (0.602610 against
  0.604063, 0.714949 against 0.712756, 0.678413 against 0.677593), and T50
  correlates more strongly with Ps30 (0.830136) and with phi (−0.815591) than
  with T24 (0.714949) or T30 (0.678413). Nothing in Fig. 5 sets the
  temperatures apart as a group.
- Pressures: P15 correlates at −0.155720 with P30 and at +0.160014 with Ps30,
  and P30 with Ps30 at −0.822805, one falling while the other rises.
- Speeds: Nf with Nc −0.032091, Nc with NRf −0.034763. The one high value, Nf
  with NRf at 0.826084, joins the physical fan speed and the same speed
  corrected for an inlet temperature that FD001 holds at the standard day's.

The figure does not support the reading the text gives it. The links the text
names may exist in the engine, but Fig. 5 is not evidence of them. Smoothing,
which takes away scatter and leaves the engines unchanged, raises T24 with T30
from 0.602610 to 0.812857 and the average size over the 91 pairs from 0.569119
to 0.693730.

**What this changes.** Nothing in the code. It rules out two claims the thesis
must not make: that a correlation in the paper's Fig. 5 measures a physical
coupling, and that a graph built from the strongest correlations encodes one.
The correlation graph of assumption A-27 joins the pairs with the least
scatter: on the raw rows the 41 strongest pairs are those of size 0.692893 and
above, 18 of them are joined in Table 3 too, and Nc and P15 are joined to
nothing. On the test engines it cannot be told apart from Table 3 (assumption
A-27).

**Limits.** The split into slow part and scatter is a model; its two checks
support it without proving it. The shares are estimated from one file, and the
ratio of 1.012246, impossible for the true values, shows how precisely. P15's
two levels do not fit the model at all. Everything here is FD001, with one
fault and one operating condition; on FD003, with two faults, the slow parts
need not move in step, and that has not been measured.

`python scripts/verify_claims.py --only noise`, which recomputes the figures
